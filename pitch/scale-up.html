Scale-up plan · 2026–2029

From one canteen
to every tray.

How PlateLoop grows from a working prototype to hundreds of canteens: prove it in one school, scale through caterers, then expand into hospitals and offices.

This is our plan to take PlateLoop from a prototype to hundreds of canteens. It has four phases: prove it in one school, grow through a caterer partner, expand into hospitals and offices, and then scale across Singapore and the region.

Phase 1 · next term

A pilot designed to prove one thing: it saves food.

How it runs

  • 1 school canteen, about 800 lunches a day
  • 2 scanners, so the queue keeps moving
  • Weeks 1–2: baseline. Scan only, no feedback, to measure today’s waste
  • Weeks 3–10: full PlateLoop. Kitchen forecasts, Loopi and class races on
  • Consent first: students and parents opt in; no photos leave the scanner

The pilot is designed to prove one thing: that PlateLoop saves food. We run two weeks of baseline, scanning without any feedback, so we know exactly how much is wasted today. Then eight weeks with the full system. Success means at least 20% less plate waste, 10% less food cooked, nine in ten trays scanned, scans under three seconds, and dishes recognised correctly at least 85% of the time.
Phase 2 · year 1

One caterer partnership opens many canteens.

Why caterers first

  • One contract, many sites: a caterer runs canteens in many schools
  • They already have to act: food waste segregation is now required for large food premises
  • They keep the savings: ingredients are their biggest cost, and measuring waste returns about 6:1

What we give them

  • Scanners on lease, nothing to buy upfront
  • One dashboard for every site: waste, orders and nutrition side by side
  • Carbon and waste reports ready for regulators and ESG reporting
  • Loopi for each school, so students help cut waste too
NEA, Resource Sustainability Act; Champions 12.3, 2018.
After the pilot, we grow through contract caterers, because one partnership opens many canteens at once. Caterers now have to separate their food waste, ingredients are their biggest cost, and measuring waste pays back about six to one. We lease them the scanners and give them one dashboard across all their sites, with carbon and waste reports ready for regulators.
Phase 3 · year 2

Same scanner. Three new places to save food.

Hospitals

Loopi Care on one ward

Run with the ward’s dietitians. Measure whether tracking every tray catches poor eaters earlier: 29% of inpatients in one Singapore study were malnourished.

Offices

Loopi Work in a corporate canteen

Pre-orders let the kitchen cook to real demand, and workers get a daily pick for their goal. Private by default.

Universities

Loopi for campuses

Large dining halls with thousands of meals a day, and students who eat too little fruit and veg: only 13.6% meet the recommendation.

Lim et al., Clinical Nutrition 2012; Chew et al., 2017.
In year two we bring the same scanner to three new places. On a hospital ward, run with the dietitians, Loopi Care can catch patients who aren't eating enough. In an office canteen, Loopi Work's pre-orders help the kitchen cook to real demand. And university dining halls serve thousands of meals a day to students who mostly don't eat enough fruit and veg.
Technology

Built to roll out, not just to demo.

Hardware

Off-the-shelf parts

Depth camera, load cell, tablet and an edge AI module. A contract manufacturer builds the casing.

New menus

Learns new dishes fast

Each canteen’s menu is known in advance, so the AI only chooses between today’s dishes. New dishes are added from labelled trays.

Cloud

One platform, many sites

Every scanner syncs grams per dish to one cloud, so a caterer sees all its canteens together. Works offline too.

Privacy

PDPA by design

No photos leave the scanner. Faces become a match code. Data is deleted on schedule, and consent comes first.

We designed PlateLoop to roll out. The hardware uses off-the-shelf parts, so a contract manufacturer can build it. The AI only has to choose between the dishes on today's menu, which keeps it fast and accurate, and new dishes are learned from labelled trays. Every scanner syncs to one cloud, so a caterer sees all its sites together. And privacy is built in: no photos ever leave the scanner.

The economics work for both sides. A school serving 800 meals a day saves about 2,400 dollars a month in ingredients and pays 470, a five-times return, so it pays back a year of the subscription in under three months. Risks

What could go wrong, and what we’ll do.

RiskHow we handle it
Mixed dishes are hard to recogniseCompartment trays, a known daily menu, and the weighing platform to check every estimate.
Students forget to scan after lunchLoopi only eats if you scan; reminders on the scanner; the kitchen sees trays still out.
Queues get slowerAbout 2 seconds a scan, and one scanner for roughly every 400 diners.
Privacy concernsNo photos leave the scanner, consent on day one, face sign-in can be switched off.
Savings smaller than expectedThe pilot measures them first; pricing stays below the measured saving.
We've thought about what could go wrong. Mixed dishes like curry are harder to recognise, so we use compartment trays, the known daily menu and the weighing platform. If students forget to scan, Loopi only eats when they do, and the kitchen can send reminders. Queues stay fast at about two seconds a scan. Privacy is protected because no photos leave the scanner. And if savings are smaller than we expect, the pilot will show it first, and we'll price below the real saving.
