Hi, we’re team PlateLoop. In one sentence: PlateLoop is an AI food scanner that measures what people are served and what they leave, so canteens cook the right amount and waste less. Today we’ll walk you through how we got there using design thinking, show you the working prototype live, and end with the business case and what we’d like from you.

Singapore threw away about 755,000 tonnes of food in 2023, and only around 18% was recycled. Worldwide, about 28% of food waste comes from food service, which is exactly the canteens and caterers we’re targeting, and food waste is 8 to 10% of global emissions. So this isn’t a small problem, and a lot of it happens in places where one kitchen feeds hundreds of people every day.

We started by looking at who is actually affected by one canteen tray. There’s the student, who gets a standard scoop and feels pressure to finish it. There’s the canteen operator, who cooks by experience with no data. In hospitals, there’s the nurse who often finds out too late that a patient hasn’t been eating. And in offices, there’s the worker with a health goal who has no idea what’s on his plate. Same tray, four very different needs.

We didn’t want to guess what each group needs, so we read the research. Students in Singapore eat very little fruit and veg, budget meals rarely balance, and counting calories can harm teenagers, so Loopi shows food groups instead, and uses class competition, which cut plate waste by a third in school studies. In hospitals, almost a third of patients are malnourished and hand-written food charts are nearly always incomplete. Letting patients choose their own meals raises intake, so Loopi Care measures every tray, lets patients choose tomorrow’s meals, and sends the patient’s own reason to the nurse. In offices, most people eat out, and heavy-carb lunches make the afternoon slump worse. Pre-ordering leads to healthier choices, so Loopi Work adds pre-ordering and a 3 pm energy check-in, and keeps everything private.

We compared five ideas. Weighing the bin is what kitchens do today, but it can’t tell you which dish or which person. Surveys are slow and people forget. A phone photo app works, but nobody will photograph every lunch. Pre-ordering helps cooking but doesn’t measure leftovers. A two-second tray scan before and after lunch, with face sign-in, was the only idea that ticked every box, including not slowing the queue.
The idea

One scanner. Two scans.

−

525 geaten, dish by dish
About 2 seconds per scanSign in by face, no cardPer person · per dish · every meal
So here’s the core idea. You scan your tray before lunch, which tells us what you were served. You scan it again after, which tells us what’s left. Served minus left equals exactly what you ate, dish by dish. Each scan takes about two seconds, and you sign in just by looking at the camera. No cards, nothing to type.

Test · what broke, and what we changed

We walked through every step until it broke.

Face not recognised

Masks and backlight left students stuck at the scanner.
“Try again” or type your class and register number.

Hand on the tray

The scale recorded a wrong weight.
It waits until the weight is steady, then measures again.

Forgot to scan back

Trays with no after scan meant missing data.
The Kitchen can put a reminder on the scanner in one tap.

Clean-plate pressure

A “finish everything” score pushed students to overeat.
Compare with your own usual; a small plate finished scores full marks.

Nobody acted on alerts

A hospital alert with no next step.
Nurse station with actions: check in, supplement drink, dietitian.

Privacy worries

“Is it taking photos of me?”
Consent on day one, face sign-in can be switched off, no photos leave.
Then we tested. We walked through every step of the prototype, looking for where a real person would get stuck. Six things broke, and each became a feature. If the face isn’t recognised, you can type your number. If your hand is still on the tray, it waits and measures again. Forgotten after scans become a one-tap reminder. We replaced any clean-plate scoring with comparing you to your own usual. Alerts now have next steps. And we added consent and a way to switch off face sign-in.

Because this involves faces and food, trust matters. No photos ever leave the scanner. Kitchens see tray numbers, not names. Every app opens with consent, and you can switch off face sign-in or hide your name. Data is deleted on a schedule. The game is designed to be kind. And whenever a number is an estimate, we say so.
