# PlateLoop pitch script

A standalone speech of about **5 minutes** (about 720 words at a relaxed pace). It works with or without slides. Paragraph breaks are natural places to pause or hand over to the next speaker.

Every figure is checked against its source in [research.md](research.md).

---

Every school day in Singapore, thousands of lunch trays go back to the kitchen half full. Nobody writes down what was left. It goes straight into the bin, and tomorrow, the kitchen cooks the same amount again.

We're [team name], and we built **PlateLoop** to break that loop.

In 2023, Singapore threw away **755,000 tonnes** of food. Only 18% of it was recycled. The rest was burnt, and the ash went to Semakau, our only landfill. Around the world, more than a quarter of all food waste comes from food service: canteens, caterers and restaurants. And wasted food causes **8 to 10%** of global greenhouse gas emissions.

But when we went and watched how lunch actually works, we realised the problem isn't that people don't care. It's that **nobody can see what's actually being eaten.**

Think about Wei Ling. She's fifteen, she gets the same big scoop as everyone else, and she's told to finish it. Think about Mrs Lim, who runs the canteen. She cooks by feel, and some days half the vegetables come back. And think about a nurse on a hospital ward, who finds out days too late that a patient has stopped eating. Three different people, one missing piece of information.

So we built one scanner that does two scans.

You look at the camera and put your tray down before you eat. After lunch, you do it again. In about two seconds, the AI **recognises every dish** on the tray, measures **how much of each one** you ate, and works out the **nutrients** you actually took in: energy, protein, carbs, fat, fibre and sodium. Six hundred and twenty grams served, ninety-five left: five hundred and twenty-five grams eaten.

This isn't science fiction. Research systems that scan trays this way, before and after the meal, already identify about **89%** of foods, and their nutrient estimates closely match the real values. We designed PlateLoop around that proven method, with a weighing platform to double-check every estimate. And no photos ever leave the scanner: faces become a match code on the device, and only grams and a tray number are sent.

That one measurement powers everything else.

For the kitchen, PlateLoop shows which dishes come back, and plans tomorrow's order from real attendance, the weather and school events. That matters, because **measuring waste cuts it.** When 86 catering sites, including some here in Singapore, started tracking their food waste, they cut it by **36% in the first year**, and got **six dollars back for every dollar** they spent. PlateLoop does that tracking automatically, on every single tray.

For students, there's **Loopi**: a virtual pet that only eats the lunch you really ate. Waste less, and it stays healthy, earns hearts and levels up. It shows food groups instead of calories, because counting calories can harm teenagers, and it rewards the right portion, never a clean plate. Classes race each week to waste the least, and in one school study, that kind of competition alone cut plate waste by **35%**.

In hospitals, almost **three in ten** patients are malnourished, and hand-written food charts miss most of it. **Loopi Care** measures the protein and energy in every meal, lets patients choose tomorrow's food, and when a meal isn't eaten, tells the nurse *why*.

And in offices, where six in ten people eat out most days, **Loopi Work** turns a health goal into a daily pick from the canteen, lets workers pre-order to skip the queue, and keeps their data private by default.

We didn't just build it; we tried to break it. When a face isn't recognised, you type your class number. When a hand is still on the tray, it waits and measures again. And every alert comes with a next step, so nothing just sits there.

So what does it add up to? For one school serving 800 meals a day, cutting waste by 30%, less than those caterers achieved, means **5.7 tonnes** of food saved, about **14 tonnes** of carbon avoided, and **S$28,500** back every year. On a monthly subscription, with the scanner leased so there's nothing to buy upfront, PlateLoop pays for itself in **under three months**.

We'd start with contract caterers, because one partnership brings many canteens at once, and then grow into hospitals and offices.

But first, we want to prove it. **Our ask is simple: one school canteen, for one term.** We'll bring the scanner and the apps, and share every number with the school and the canteen operator.

PlateLoop. **Know what's eaten. Waste less.**

Thank you.

## Likely judge questions

- **How accurate is it?** Research systems using the same before-and-after depth-camera method identify about 89% of foods, and their nutrient estimates closely match the real values (Pfisterer et al., *JMIR Aging* 2022; Lu et al., 2020). The weighing platform checks the camera's estimate in real grams.
- **Won't it slow the queue?** Each scan takes about two seconds, and sign-in is by face, so there's no card to find.
- **What about privacy?** No photos leave the scanner. Faces become a match code on the device, face sign-in can be switched off, and kitchens see tray numbers, not names.
- **Where do the savings come from?** Kitchens that measure their waste cut it: 36% in the first year across 86 catering sites (Champions 12.3, 2018). Our estimate assumes a smaller 30% cut, to be proven in the pilot.
- **Why would students bother?** Because Loopi only grows from the lunch they really eat, and their class is racing. Waste less, and their pet thrives.
- **What does it cost?** From S$200 a site a month (illustrative), with the scanner leased, so there's nothing to buy upfront.
