# What the people we design for need

Desk research behind Loopi, Loopi Care and Loopi Work: what each group struggles with at meals, what the evidence says works, and what PlateLoop does about it. Sources are at the bottom of each section. Figures are from published studies and national surveys; check the originals before quoting them.

## Students (secondary school and university)

**What we found**
- Young Singaporeans eat very little fruit, vegetables and whole grain. Among university students, only 13.6% met the international recommendation for fruit and vegetables and 27.1% met the national one [1]. Among children, only 14% (whole grains), 16% (fruit) and 21% (vegetables) met the recommendations [2].
- Budget meals rarely balance. A sample of 40 budget meals across Singapore found none met My Healthy Plate; under a price ceiling meals become carb-heavy and short on protein [3].
- Counting calories can harm young people. Regular users of diet and fitness apps show more disordered-eating symptoms, and apps for adolescents are advised to avoid highlighting calorie counts [4].
- Friendly competition works. Letting students weigh their own waste and posting class results cut plate waste by 35%, and gamification motivated nearly 90% of students in one campus study [5].

**What PlateLoop does**
- Loopi shows **food groups, not calories**, by default: did your plate have grains, protein, vegetables and fruit? Calories stay hidden unless a student chooses to see them.
- **Try-it quests** reward tasting the vegetable of the week, and Loopi grows from finishing a right-sized portion, never from a clean-plate rule.
- **Class challenges** turn waste into a weekly race between classes, with names only if students opt in.
- The **before scan** lets students ask for less rice and still get full credit, so a cheap meal can still be a balanced one.

Sources: [1] Chew et al., *Asia Pac J Public Health* 2017, doi:10.1177/1010539517696553. [2] Evaluation of Singaporean children's diets against guidelines, PMC6893683. [3] CNA sampling of budget meals, via NUS SPH press, 2024. [4] Flinders University systematic review of diet and fitness apps, 2025; E4W adolescent app trial, PMC11650080. [5] Strategies to reduce plate waste in schools, PMC10271086; FoodWise campus gamification study, 2023.

## Hospital patients (and the nurses and dietitians who look after them)

**What we found**
- About **29% of inpatients** in a major Singapore tertiary hospital were malnourished. They stayed longer (6.9 vs 4.6 days), were more likely to be readmitted within 15 days, and had a higher risk of death [6].
- Hospital plate waste is high: typically **15–35%**, median around **31%** of food served [7].
- Food charts filled in by staff are unreliable: **93% were incomplete** in one study, estimates were right less than 45% of the time, and staff missed more than half of the residents eating 75% or less [8].
- Patients leave food because of **poor appetite, taste changes, tiredness and pain**, portions that feel too big (43% said so in one survey), limited choice, and trays that are hard to open or reach [9].
- Letting patients **choose their own meals close to mealtime** raises energy and protein intake and cuts plate waste and cost; a review of five studies (720 patients) found the same [10].

**What PlateLoop does**
- The ward scanner measures **every tray automatically**, so nobody has to estimate a food chart and no poor meal goes unnoticed.
- After two poor meals, the **nurse station** gets an alert with the patient's own reason (appetite, taste, too big, tired, pain, nausea) and a one-tap response.
- **Choose tomorrow's meals**: patients pick their dishes and a portion size the day before, in large text, which is exactly what the bedside-ordering studies show works.
- A **recovery ring** tracks protein, the nutrient that matters most for healing, and a **read-aloud** button helps patients who struggle with small text.

Sources: [6] Lim et al., *Clinical Nutrition* 2012, 31(3):345–350. [7] Food waste in hospitals: review, 2019; systematic review of hospital plate waste, *Nutrients* 2023, doi:10.3390/nu15020301. [8] Accuracy of food intake charts completed by nursing staff, *Clinical Nutrition* 2015; Simmons et al., nursing home intake monitoring, *J Am Geriatr Soc* 2000. [9] Managing food waste in the inpatient population, PMC10749062; why elderly inpatients don't eat adequately, PMC12892646. [10] Bedside Menu Ordering System, *Clin Nutr ESPEN* 2018; systematic review of electronic bedside meal ordering, PMC7383857.

## Office workers

**What we found**
- **Six in ten** Singapore residents usually eat out for lunch or dinner (1 in 2 in 2004), and people who eat out tend to eat more and less healthily [11].
- Obesity is rising: from **10.5%** of residents in 2019–20 to **12.7%** in 2023–24 [12].
- The **post-lunch dip** between 1 and 3 pm is natural, but high-carbohydrate lunches make it worse; people who eat lower-glycaemic, higher-protein lunches keep better focus through the afternoon [13].
- **Pre-ordering** lunch at a workplace canteen leads to healthier choices and less waste; nudges in a pre-order system gave **51% more fruit** and **30% more vegetable** orders, and 92% of employees found pre-ordering acceptable [14].
- Workers worry about sharing health data with their employer: more than half are hesitant, and a quarter would not share it at all [15].

**What PlateLoop does**
- Loopi Work turns a **personal goal** into a daily pick from the canteen lines, with small tweaks such as adding eggs for protein.
- **Pre-order** the pick before 11:30 to skip the queue, and the kitchen cooks to the pre-orders instead of guessing.
- A **3 pm energy check-in** shows each worker how their lunch choices line up with their afternoon energy.
- Everything is **private by default**: the employer sees only anonymous canteen totals and team challenges, never a person's meals.
