PlateLoop

Our AI food scanner automatically analyses the type and amount of food served, eaten and left, so cafeteria managers know what people actually like, order the right amount of ingredients, and waste less food and money.

What PlateLoop does

Each person signs in with their face and scans their tray before and after the meal. The difference is exactly what they ate, dish by dish.

Up to 30%
lower food material cost

We predict future consumption, so the kitchen prepares and orders only what will be eaten.

less plate waste

Live
greenhouse gas saved

Every tray shows how much CO₂ it saved, so people see their contribution and leave less.

How it works

Everything heavy happens on the scanner. Photos never leave it: only grams per dish and a tray number go to the cloud, where every app reads from the same numbers.

One tray, one lunch

Two scans of about two seconds each. Nobody types anything or carries a card.

  1. ServeTake a tray and ask for more or less of each dish.
  2. Scan beforeLook at the camera, put the tray down. That's what you were served.
  3. EatSit with friends. Nothing to do.
  4. Scan afterSame scanner. Now it sees what's left.
  5. ScrapeLeftovers go into the food waste bin, which weighs them too.
  6. ReturnTray on the rack. The apps update in seconds.

When something goes wrong

A queue of hungry students can't wait for a stuck machine. Every step has a way out, and you can try each one in the Kiosk demo.

Face not found
Mask, cap or backlight

“We couldn't find you.” Try again, or type a class and register number on the keypad.

Nothing is saved from a failed match
Opted out
No face sign-in

Students can switch face sign-in off in the app. The scanner goes straight to the number keypad for them.

Their match code is deleted
Hand on the tray
The weight keeps changing

The scale sees it's still moving and asks for hands off, then measures again by itself.

No wrong numbers get recorded
Skipped the first scan
Only an after scan

A regular portion is used as the estimate, the result is marked “estimated”, and the student is told why.

The Kitchen can see which trays are estimates
Trays still out
People forget to scan back

The Kitchen sees how many trays are missing their after scan and can put a reminder on the scanner screen.

One tap in Needs attention
Wi-Fi drops
Offline

The AI runs on the scanner, so scanning never stops. Results wait in a queue and sync when the connection is back.

By design: no cloud needed to scan

Privacy by design

No photos leave the scanner

Only grams per dish and a tray number are sent. Faces become a match code on the device.

Kitchens see trays, not names

Staff work with tray numbers. A form teacher or nurse sees names only where there's a care reason.

Consent on day one

Every app opens with what's recorded, who sees it and switches for face sign-in and leaderboard names.

Lunch Rush

Spend a lunch break in a Singapore school canteen. Take a tray, tell Mrs Lim how much of each dish you want, and scan it at the real PlateLoop scanner, read up close. Then eat with your friends, scan again, scrape your leftovers into its food waste bin and return your tray. People talk to you, and the canteen is full of sound. Your tray shows up in the other apps too.

Play
  1. Play Lunch Rush to see a whole lunch from the student's eyes, or open PlateLoop Prototype, tap the screen's light and Try the kiosk: pick Wei Ling and let her walk up before lunch, then after.
  2. Open Loopi: her lunch is in Loopi's bowl. Feed it, then sweep up the crumbs from what she left.
  3. Open Kitchen: the tray is in the live feed, and Carbon shows the whole cafeteria's footprint.
  4. Open Loopi Care or Loopi Work and scan a meal from the demo panel to see the healthcare report change. In Loopi Work, change the goal and watch the targets and the daily pick change with it.
